What We Believe

Simple, and centered on Christ

A short set of convictions that shape this fellowship. Christ at the center, love as the expression of faith, and nothing added that Jesus never asked for.

Only Christ Is Holy

He alone is holy

No person, place, book, or building is holy in itself. Only Christ is holy. Scripture is a collection of human writings that points us toward Him; it helps reveal the world He entered and the way He taught His followers to live, but it is not an object of worship. We honor Christ, we learn from His words, and we measure everything — including our own traditions — against the life He actually lived.

No Tithing Required

Jesus never asked you to tithe

Jesus never asked you to tithe. Nowhere in His teaching did He set a percentage, demand a payment, or tie your standing with God to money. There is no demand for you to give in any capacity. The tithe belonged to an old system of law and temple that Christ Himself moved beyond. What He asked for instead was a heart turned toward others: feed the hungry, help the poor, care for the sick, welcome the stranger, forgive freely.

Giving, in the way Christ taught it, is never a requirement for belonging to Him. It is simply love in action. When it is real, it flows from compassion rather than obligation, and it is measured by the heart behind it, not the amount. Jesus praised the widow who gave two small coins over the wealthy who gave for attention, because generosity is about love, not arithmetic.

Many institutions have turned giving into a rule, a quota, or a weekly demand, and some even teach that God’s blessings can be purchased. None of this came from Jesus. God’s favor is not for sale, and no gift can earn what Christ freely gives. Any act of generosity should come from kindness and a desire to help others — never from guilt or expectation.

Simply Christian Fellowship will never pressure anyone to give. There is no collection here, no expectation, and no guilt. If a person feels moved to help someone in need, that is between them and Christ, and it belongs wherever it does the most good — feeding people, meeting real needs, and carrying His compassion further into the world.

Following Christ Through Action

Faith without works is lost

Faith is not something we claim. It is something we live. Jesus said the world would know His followers not by their words, their titles, or their beliefs spoken aloud, but by their love. Faith without works is dead. A faith that never reaches another person, never feeds anyone, never forgives, never lifts a burden, is only an idea.

We are not Christians because we say we are. We are Christians when we live the way Christ taught: when we love our enemies, bless those who curse us, help the poor, feed the hungry, sit with the lonely, and welcome the ones others turn away. These are not lofty spiritual achievements. They are small, daily, ordinary acts of love — and they are the whole of it.

Following Christ through action means letting compassion cost us something: our time, our comfort, our pride. It means choosing mercy when judgment is easier, and service when recognition is more tempting. This is the narrow path, and it is walked one kind act at a time.

No Infallible Human Intermediaries

No one stands between you and Christ

No person stands between you and Christ. You do not need a pastor’s permission, a priest’s blessing, a title, an office, or an institution to know Him. Jesus tore down that wall. He spoke directly to fishermen, tax collectors, outcasts, and strangers, and He invited every one of them to follow — with no gatekeeper in the way.

No man is created above another. Leaders can teach, encourage, and serve, but no human being is holy, infallible, or closer to God by rank. When people are told they must go through a particular person or institution to reach Christ, something has been added that Jesus never asked for. He is not far off, waiting behind a hierarchy. He is near to anyone who seeks Him honestly.

Simply Christian Fellowship has no clergy set above its members, no chain of authority, and no one whose word is beyond question. We learn together, we correct one another gently, and we hold only Christ as holy. Every voice here is a fellow traveler, never a master.
