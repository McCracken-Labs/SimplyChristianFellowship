To Follow Is Simply to Love

This week the fellowship comes back to the one line that holds the whole thing together, that to follow Christ is simply to love.

This Week’s Devotional

❦

“A new commandment I give unto you, That ye love one another; as I have loved you, that ye also love one another.”John 13:34, KJV

Jesus did not leave his friends a doctrine to master or a title to defend. On the night before he died, with everything closing in around him, he gave them one plain instruction. Love one another, the way I have loved you. He said this knowing that within hours they would scatter, that one of them would swear he never knew him. The love he asked for looked like a basin of water and a towel, and the willingness to keep showing up for people who kept getting it wrong.

This is why the fellowship keeps returning to one sentence: to follow the teachings of Christ is simply to love. That kind of love shows up in your hands and your time, given to people who may never thank you for it. You do not need a title or a role to offer it. You only need to notice who is in front of you today and choose them.

Practice. Today, do one small, practical kindness for someone you find hard to love, and do it without telling anyone about it.

May you love the person in front of you the way you have been loved, plainly and without condition. Go in peace.

John tells us this happened at supper, after Jesus had already tied a towel around his waist and washed the feet of the same men who would leave him within the day. He calls it a new commandment, though love itself was nothing new. The Hebrew scriptures had already told people to love their neighbor as themselves, and that command was old and familiar by the time Jesus sat down at that table. Jesus raised the measure, love one another as I have loved you, and that moves the standard from something reasonable and self-referencing to something with no ceiling, since his love for his friends did not stop at their failures. He washed the feet of a man who would betray him within hours. He kept loving a friend who would deny him three times before the sun came up. That is the shape of the love he asked them to carry forward.

A Commandment, Not a Suggestion

There is a reason Jesus called this a commandment and not a suggestion. A suggestion can be admired and set aside. A commandment asks something of you whether you feel like giving it or not, on the days you are tired, on the days the person in front of you has done nothing to earn your patience. This fellowship's starting point is that Christ alone is holy. No book or building carries that holiness on its own, and no title does either. Following him here means doing what he asked, loving the people around us the way he loved his friends, including the ones who were about to let him down, more than it means memorizing the right words about him.

This is also why the fellowship does not ask for tithes or attach conditions to belonging. Love, as Jesus described it, was never a transaction. He did not love his disciples because they had proven themselves reliable. He loved them the night before they failed him completely. If that is the pattern, then love here cannot be earned by attendance or by getting the doctrine right, and it cannot be bought by giving either. It is simply given, the way it was given to you, and then it becomes someone else's turn to receive it. No offering plate changes that arrangement.

Love as the Whole Shape of Following

People come to this fellowship carrying all kinds of history with religion. Some were told they had to believe correctly before they could belong, and that any honest question was a kind of failure. Others are not sure what they believe about Christ at all, and wonder if that uncertainty disqualifies them from anything here. None of that disqualifies anyone. The commandment was never about having your beliefs settled first. It was about showing up for the person next to you, and that is available whether your faith feels solid or still forming, whether you walked in today sure of everything or sure of almost nothing.

So the practice this week is not complicated, and it does not require a theology degree or a perfect track record. Someone in your life is hard to love right now, maybe a coworker or a family member you have been keeping at arm's length. Love one another as I have loved you was spoken to people who had already begun to fail at it, and Jesus did not wait for them to improve before loving them first. Pick that person, and reach out today with one plain, ordinary act of care, maybe just a phone call that says you have been thinking of them.
