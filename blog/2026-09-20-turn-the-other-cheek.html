Turn the Other Cheek

This week's reflection sits with one of Jesus' hardest teachings, the call to meet harm without striking back.

This Week’s Devotional

❦

“But I say unto you, That ye resist not evil: but whosoever shall smite thee on thy right cheek, turn to him the other also.”Matthew 5:39, KJV

Jesus is talking about the moment right after someone has hurt you, the instant your body wants to hit back. He tells his followers to let that instant pass without answering blow for blow. Revenge rarely ends anything on its own. It hands the hurt to someone else and waits for its turn to come back around.

Turning the other cheek is a way of refusing to let someone else's cruelty decide who you become. You can walk away or ask someone for help and still be living this teaching, since neither of those is revenge. Your own hand is what changes here. Your safety belongs to a different conversation, one to have with someone you trust before anything else.

Practice. Today, when you feel the flash of wanting to snap back at someone, pause for one breath before you respond.

May you find the strength to set down what was done to you, and the peace of knowing your worth was never in question. May love outlast whatever tried to end it.

"Turn the other cheek" sits in the middle of the Sermon on the Mount, right after Jesus tells his listeners not to swear oaths and right before he tells them to love their enemies. Read together, this section is about how people treat each other once someone has already crossed a line. A slap on the cheek in that culture was rarely meant to injure. It was an insult, a way of putting someone in their place. Jesus tells his followers to absorb the insult and offer the other cheek rather than answer with a slap of their own. He is naming the exact moment when most people reach for payback, and asking for something else instead. The insult still happened, the sting is still real, and nobody is asked to pretend otherwise. What he asks is that a person's own next move stay their own, rather than something the harm gets to decide for them.

Breaking the cycle

Revenge has a strange logic to it. It always feels proportional from the inside, a fair response to what was done to you, but it rarely looks that way from the outside, and it almost never stays even. One hit becomes two. One sharp word becomes a feud that outlives the people who started it. Families carry grudges for decades. Neighbors stop speaking over an argument nobody quite remembers the start of. Jesus is pointing at this pattern and asking his followers to be the ones who stop it, even when stopping it costs them something. Turning the other cheek takes a kind of strength that rarely gets noticed, the strength to feel the urge for payback and choose not to act on it.

Not a reason to stay in danger

This teaching gets misused sometimes, especially toward people already being hurt by someone who has power over them. Telling someone in an abusive relationship or situation to simply keep offering the other cheek causes real harm, and it stretches this passage past what it was ever meant to carry. Jesus is talking about revenge here, about the instinct to strike back at an insult or a wrong. Enduring ongoing violence or control is a separate matter entirely, one this passage never addresses. If you are in danger, please reach out to someone you trust or a local support line. Safety comes first, before anything else asked of you here.

This teaching makes more sense once you set aside the idea that following Christ means following an institution. Nothing about this fellowship asks you to treat a book, a building, or a person as holy in itself. Only Christ is holy, and what he asks for here lands less as a rule to obey and more as a change in what you do with your own hands and your own next move. There is no tithe attached to this teaching and no pressure behind it. It shows up in small places, an argument with someone you love or a rude reply typed and almost sent.

You don't have to feel ready for this teaching to try it. Start with something small, a comment you don't have to answer, a slight you can let pass without needing the last word. Notice what happens in your chest when you don't take the bait. If this teaching sits uneasily with you, if part of you wants to argue with Jesus about fairness, that's a fine place to start too. Bring the doubt. Sit with the discomfort a while and see what it teaches you, one ordinary Tuesday at a time.
