Only Christ Is Holy

No book, no building, no person is holy on its own. Holiness belongs to God, and it came near to us in one person.

This Week’s Devotional

❦

“Why callest thou me good? none is good, save one, that is, God.”Luke 18:19, KJV

A man once ran up to Jesus, knelt in the road, and called him Good Teacher. Jesus stopped him on the first word. Why do you call me good? No one is good but God alone. He was not brushing off the honor. He was aiming it past himself, toward its source, the way he always did.

We reach for holy things. A gold cross, a leather Bible worn soft at the corners, a room with light coming through colored glass, a name we were taught to say with lowered eyes. Any of these can help us pray. None of them is holy on its own. Holiness is not a quality that seeps into an object because we have handled it with care. It belongs to God, and it drew near to us in one person you can actually follow.

There is relief in that once you feel it. You are not left guarding a shelf of sacred objects, afraid you have touched them wrongly. You are asked to walk with someone who is near you right now.

Practice. Today, notice one thing you have quietly treated as holy, an object, a habit, a place. Hold it a little more loosely, and turn that same care toward a person in front of you instead.

May you hold every holy thing lightly, and hold on to Christ. And may the love you have been given find its way into someone else’s ordinary day.

Ask a room full of people what is holy and you will get a pile of objects. A book. A building. A day of the week. A relic behind glass. A person in special clothing. Most of us were handed a short list of sacred things before we were old enough to question it, and we have carried the list around ever since, a little afraid of dropping it.

Jesus traveled light. He consecrated no headquarters and owned no relics. When a man called him good, he deflected the word and pointed it at God. Again and again he took holiness out of the temple, out of the hands of the professionals, and put it back where it belonged, with the Father, and with himself as the one who came to show us the Father. Only Christ is holy. Everything else on the list is a help or a distraction, depending on what we do with it.

The book that became an idol

Take the Bible, since it sits near the center of that list for so many people. It is a remarkable library, written by many hands across a thousand years, and it carries us back into the world Jesus stepped into and shows how he taught people to live. Read it. Wrestle with it. Let it correct you. Something goes wrong, though, when the book itself becomes the thing we worship. People end up defending the pages more fiercely than they follow the person the pages point to. You can memorize whole chapters, win every argument about them, and still walk past a hungry man without slowing down. Jesus met people like that. He told the scripture experts of his day that they searched the texts endlessly, sure the words held eternal life, and still they would not come to him to receive it.

And the rest of the list

The same holds for everything else we call sacred. A cross is two pieces of wood or metal. It can remind you of love poured out, or it can shrink into a lucky charm and a stick for shaming other people. A building is a roof. It can shelter a meal for the poor, or it can swallow the money that should have fed them. A Sunday is a good day to rest and gather, and it is no holier than the Tuesday you spend caring for your kids. None of these things is evil. They only turn into a problem when we let them take the place that belongs to Christ.

Why this sets you free

Holding this clearly frees you in a way that is hard to describe until it happens to you. If holiness lived inside objects and rituals, faith would be an anxious business of handling sacred things correctly and never quite trusting that you had. Since holiness lives in a person who is near and kind, faith becomes a friendship instead of a performance. You cannot fumble Christ into rejecting you. You can only keep turning back toward him and letting him change how you treat the people around you.

So keep your Bible, and read it more, not less. Keep the cross on the wall if it helps you remember. Just hold all of it with an open hand, and save your worship for the one it was always pointing to. The book cannot feed your neighbor. Christ will send you out to do it yourself.
