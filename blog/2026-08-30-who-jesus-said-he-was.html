Who Jesus Said He Was

This week's reflection looks at a claim Jesus made about himself, and what it means to follow a person rather than master an idea.

This Week’s Devotional

❦

“I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”John 14:6, KJV

Jesus never asked people to master a philosophy or memorize a rulebook about him. He asked them to follow him. When he said he was the way, the truth, and the life, he was describing a relationship, something you enter and walk in over time. A road teaches you as you travel it, and following Jesus works the same way, day after day, mile after mile.

This is good news if faith has ever felt like a test you kept failing. You do not need every answer settled before you take a step toward him. You can carry real doubt and still follow. You can be unsure about many things and still walk toward someone who calls himself the truth. Following him shows up in small moments, in a conversation you have today, a decision you make tomorrow, a habit you keep the day after that.

Practice. Today, spend five minutes talking to Jesus the way you would talk to a friend walking beside you.

May you feel Jesus walking with you today, close enough to notice. Go in his peace.

The night before he was arrested, Jesus sat with his closest friends and told them he was going away to prepare a place for them. It was an unfinished, nervous kind of conversation, the sort friends have when they are frightened and trying not to show it. Thomas, never one to nod along without asking a real question, said he did not even know where Jesus was going, so how could he know the way there. Jesus answered him plainly, telling Thomas that he himself was the way and the truth and the life, and that no one reaches the Father except through him. Thomas wanted directions. Jesus gave him himself.

A Person to Walk With

This changes what following him looks like from day to day. Picture faith as passing an exam on doctrine, and following Jesus turns into memorizing the right definitions and hoping you get the questions right when it counts. Some churches have taught it that way for centuries, and plenty of people have walked away from faith entirely after being told that correct belief was all that counted. Jesus asked fishermen and tax collectors and doubting friends to walk with him and watch him and slowly become like him. None of them passed an exam first.

You can see this in how the earliest followers wrote about their own faith. They did not write systematic essays defending Jesus as an abstract truth claim. They wrote about meals shared and storms calmed, about a friend who kept showing up even after they had failed him. Following Jesus today can look the same way. It can look like calling someone who is lonely, or forgiving a debt you were owed, or sitting with a person in a hospital room when there is nothing useful to say. None of that requires a title or a pulpit. It looks like showing up at someone's door with dinner because you heard they had a hard week.

Room for Doubt

None of this makes doubt unwelcome here. Thomas asked his honest question, and Jesus answered him without scolding him for not already knowing. If you have ever sat in a service wondering whether any of it was true, or walked away from a church because the answers you were given felt hollow, that same door is still open to you. People who carry real uncertainty and take the next step anyway have had a place in this story since the beginning.

This is part of why this fellowship does not treat any book or building as holy in itself. The Bible points toward Jesus, and a church building can gather people around him, but neither one carries holiness on its own. Only Christ is holy. A person can read every page of scripture and still miss the one it points to. A person can also walk into a plain room with no steeple and meet him there just the same. This fellowship does not ask for money as proof of devotion either, since a collection plate is not holy any more than a building is. Someone can give nothing this month and still be following Jesus closely.

You do not need your theology sorted out before you take a step toward him. Take that step today, toward a person who already knows your doubts and your failures and has not backed away from you because of them. That step gets walked one ordinary day at a time, in a phone call you make or an old habit you set down. Jesus is already present for the next one you take.
