Scripture as a Window, Not an Idol

This week we look at why we read the Bible at all, and why we were never meant to stop at the page.

This Week’s Devotional

❦

“Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of me. And ye will not come to me, that ye might have life.”John 5:39-40, KJV

Jesus said this to people who took their scriptures seriously, who had spent lifetimes studying them line by line. He was naming something easy to miss even for someone that careful. You can search a page for years and still walk right past the person it was written about. They kept searching the page while he stood right there in front of them.

That can happen just as easily now. You can love a verse, defend a translation, know the concordance backward, and still have never turned your face toward Christ. Reading it well means letting it hand you off, page by page, to a living person who is asking for your attention today, in the room you are standing in right now.

Practice. Today, before you read a verse, notice one thing it shows you about Jesus, then go do one concrete act of love because of it.

May these pages be a window you keep looking through, until Christ himself becomes clear on the other side. Go gently, and let what you see move your hands as much as your mind.

Ask people who grew up in church what they were taught about the Bible, and you will hear a familiar teaching, that the Bible is without error and settles every question. Ask people who grew up outside church, or who left it, and you often hear something else. The Bible was used against them, quoted at them, waved in their face during an argument they never wanted to have. Both experiences point to the same confusion. Somewhere along the way, the book became the object of devotion instead of the doorway to it.

This week's reading comes from a moment when Jesus was talking to religious leaders who knew their scriptures better than almost anyone alive. They studied the texts for years, argued over their meaning, built their whole identity around them. Jesus tells them plainly that they search the scriptures because they believe eternal life is found there, and he agrees the scriptures do testify of him. Then he says the hard part out loud. You search these pages and still will not come to me. All that study, and they missed the person standing right in front of them, in the room, at that moment.

A Window to Look Through

The Bible is a collection of writings, gathered over centuries, written by people wrestling with God, with suffering, with hope, with each other. Poems, letters, histories, prophecies, songs, some borrowed and reworked from older traditions around them. Some of it is beautiful. Some of it is difficult, even troubling, and we do not pretend otherwise. What holds all of it together, for us, is that it keeps pointing past itself, toward a person. A window works because you can see through it. Nobody hangs a window on the wall and admires the glass forever without ever looking at what is outside.

It is easy to drift into treating the book itself as sacred. You can spend years defending a translation, memorizing verses for debate, correcting someone's interpretation online, and never once let any of it change how you treat your neighbor. That drift happens quietly, especially when you feel certain you are right. The Pharisees in that story read carefully and still missed what all that reading was for. We can do the same thing with a different set of verses and a different argument, and still be standing in the same spot they were standing in.

Following the Person

Christ never asked anyone to worship a scroll or defend a canon. He asked people to follow him, to feed someone who was hungry, to sit with someone who was grieving, to forgive someone who had wronged them. Following him starts with looking through the window at the person on the other side of it, and walking toward him with your actual hands and your actual time. The Bible helps with that. It tells stories about who he was, what he did, and how people responded to him, badly and well. But knowing a story about someone and knowing that person are two different things, the way reading a letter from a friend is different from sitting across the table from them.

If you have been hurt by people who used the Bible as a weapon, or if you have drifted away because you got tired of arguments over pages instead of a person, you are welcome here. You do not need to have the book figured out to belong. You do not need to defend every line of it or explain the parts that trouble you. Open it this week to whatever page draws you toward him, and go from there, slowly, at your own pace, doubts and all.
