About Us

A sincere walk with Christ

Simply Christian Fellowship exists to help people follow Jesus in a clear, simple, and sincere way, free from guilt, pressure, or hierarchy.

Simply Christian Fellowship exists to help people follow Jesus in a clear, simple, and sincere way. This is a place for anyone who wants to focus on Christ without the added weight of religious systems, traditions, or expectations. The goal is to support people who seek an honest relationship with Christ, free from guilt, pressure, or hierarchy.

Many people love Christ but feel disconnected from organized religion. Some have experienced confusion, burnout, or harm in institutional settings. Others want a faith that is direct, peaceful, and centered on the teachings of Jesus Himself. Simply Christian Fellowship offers a calm space for learning, reflection, and connection for anyone walking this path.

Scripture as a window, not an idol

Jesus never pointed to a book, a building, a relic, or a person and called it holy. He pointed to God, and He lived in a way that showed people what God is like. In His life, holiness was never a quality that belonged to objects or places. It belonged to Him.

Scripture is a collection of writings from many people across many centuries. It gives a glimpse of the world before Christ and the world His arrival reshaped, moving from a long history of laws, customs, and human struggle toward the clarity of the life described in the New Testament. These writings carry real value, and they help reveal the world Jesus entered and the way He taught His followers to live. None of them is holy in itself. Through the New Testament authors we come to see His character, His compassion, and the direction He gave the people who followed Him.

Some have drifted into treating the Bible itself as the thing to be worshipped, bowing to the pages instead of following the person the pages describe. A book can be memorized, quoted, and defended without a single one of Christ’s teachings ever reaching your hands. Jesus warned about this in His own day. He told the religious experts that they searched the scriptures endlessly, sure they held life inside them, and still they would not come to Him for the life they were looking for.

Giving is love, never a requirement

We reject the idea that money measures where you stand with God. Christ never taught tithing. He did not ask anyone to hand a percentage to a temple, a church, or a religion, and He never tied a person’s worth or salvation to the amount they gave. What He asked for, and often plainly commanded, was mercy carried out by hand. Feed the hungry. Welcome the stranger and the immigrant. Clothe the poor and sit with the sick and the imprisoned. In Matthew 25 He measures a whole life by these acts, and says that whatever we did for the least among us, we did for Him.

Some people argue that helping the poor only rewards laziness, and they reach for scripture to excuse turning away. Spend a little time among the homeless in America and that argument falls apart. Most of the people sleeping outside are not idle. They are carrying untreated mental illness, addiction, deep trauma, or a collapse of circumstances that left them with nowhere to turn. Jesus ran no means test before He healed or fed anyone. He met the need in front of Him, and He told us to do the same.

Giving, then, is love with skin on it. When it is real it comes freely, with no pressure and no guilt, and it goes where it actually relieves suffering.

A shared walk, not an institution

Simply Christian Fellowship is not an organization built on titles or positions, and it does not hold any person, place, or object as holy. No one is created above anyone else. This is a space to learn, grow, and reconnect with Christ honestly. As it grows, more voices and experiences will shape it, while the foundation stays the same: Christ at the center, and love as the way faith gets lived out.

Faith that never turns into action is not really faith. You can call yourself a Christian the same way you can call yourself an astronaut. The title only means something once you have actually gone where it says you go. An astronaut has to get past fifty miles up. Someone who follows Christ has to actually love people, feed them, forgive them, and carry their burdens. Jesus said you would know His followers by their fruit.

Fellowship Unifies

Held together by love, not sameness

Fellowship is simply people walking toward Christ together. It does not require agreement on every question, membership in an organization, or sameness of background, only shared honesty and shared love.

For too long, faith has been used to divide people by denomination, by politics, by who is in and who is out. Jesus moved in the opposite direction. He ate with the excluded, crossed every social line of His day, and prayed that His followers would be one. For Him, unity meant love strong enough to hold difference without fear, not uniformity.

Here, fellowship means you are welcome as you are, with your questions, your doubts, and your story. We come from different places and see many things differently, and that is not a threat. What holds us together is a way to walk together, not a creed to sign: Christ at the center, compassion in practice, and love as the language we share.

Simply Christian Fellowship
