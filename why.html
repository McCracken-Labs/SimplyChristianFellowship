Why Simply Christian

Walking away from what religion became, not from Christ

Across the world, newer generations are stepping away from organized religion. Christ is not the problem. Many of the institutions built in His name are.

Across the world, newer generations are stepping away from organized religion. This shift is not driven by a lack of belief in Jesus. Many people still admire Christ, trust His teachings, and desire a spiritual life shaped by love, humility, and compassion. Christ is not the problem. Many institutions built in His name have drifted far from Him, often reflecting power, money, division, and politics more than the life of Jesus.

Younger generations have watched churches promote fear instead of hope, judgment instead of mercy, and authority instead of service. They have seen pastors preaching prosperity while members struggle to feed their families. Religious leaders live in wealth while using Scripture to demand more giving. Politics gets preached from pulpits, treating neighbors as enemies and dividing communities based on ideology. This is not the way of Jesus. He taught people to love their enemies, bless those who curse them, help the poor, feed the hungry, heal the broken, and welcome the outcast.

People today are walking away because they want honesty and humility. They want the teachings of Christ without the corruption of systems that seek money and power. Faith, to them, looks like Jesus overturning the tables of those who used religion for profit. And they want a community shaped by His compassion, one that isn't driven by control or political influence.

Prosperity for whom?

Prosperity preaching is one of the clearest examples of this corruption. Jesus never promised financial gain, tied blessings to donations, or measured faith by money. Yet many churches pressure people to give with threats of spiritual failure or promises of earthly reward. This practice places heavy burdens on people, the same kind Jesus condemned. He praised the widow who gave almost nothing. The wealthy who gave for attention did not receive the same praise. He taught that God’s will was reflected in serving the poor rather than in enriching leaders.

Faith is not a political weapon

Church involvement in political battles has driven many away as well. Instead of showing grace to all people, some institutions place ideology above the teachings of Christ. They use faith to justify anger, division, and exclusion. Jesus never aligned Himself with political power. He consistently challenged those who sought authority. He called His followers to love without condition and to build unity rather than division.

Younger generations see these contradictions clearly. Faith gets used to control instead of to heal. Religion gets used to exclude instead of to welcome. Churches protect their image instead of protecting the vulnerable. And so they walk away from systems that no longer resemble Jesus, even while their love for Him remains.

Simply Christian Fellowship exists for those who still believe in the teachings of Jesus but feel disconnected from institutions that no longer reflect His way. It offers a path back to what matters, setting aside politics, profit, and hierarchy to make room for Christ, compassion, and community. Love shapes the life that grows there.

Simply Christian Fellowship

A shared, sincere walk in Christ, free of pressure, with Christ at the center and love as how faith gets lived out.
